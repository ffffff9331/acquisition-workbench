# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v2
**Last updated:** 2026-08-24

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** A local-demand acquisition playbook and experiment workspace for bathroom and balcony-cabinet retailers, focused on store visits and measurement appointments from renovation and partial-refit demand.

**What it does:** The product turns a retailer's goals, local market, product category, budget, and assets into a specific acquisition campaign. It generates an execution pack, gives the campaign a traceable lead entry point, captures a small amount of lead-quality feedback, and turns the result into a reusable playbook.

**Product category:** Vertical lead-acquisition operations / campaign delivery workspace.

**Product type:** B2B SaaS with an initial implementation and co-creation service.

**Business model:** Setup and playbook-migration fee, recurring subscription for active client workspaces, and optional vertical template packs or implementation services.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:**
- Primary early users: local bathroom retailers and balcony-cabinet customisation businesses with a consultation, store-visit, or measurement-led sales process, and an active willingness to invest in local content, community activity, referrals, or paid acquisition.
- Primary scale buyers: short-video agencies, media-buying agencies, and marketing-service teams serving those retailers.

**Decision-makers:** Store owner, regional dealer, marketing lead, agency owner, or agency delivery lead.

**Primary use case:** Plan and run a repeatable local acquisition campaign that turns local homeowner attention into a qualified consultation, store visit, or measurement appointment.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Decide which acquisition action is worth running this week.
- Turn a proven tactic into localised content, an offer, lead capture, and a clear execution checklist.
- Show whether an activity produced qualified leads and at what cost.
- Preserve successful and failed campaign conditions so the next campaign does not restart from scratch.

**Use cases:**
- Short-video content plus a size, budget, or room-planning consultation offer.
- New-community or local-area campaign.
- Designer, installer, renovation-company, or complementary-store referral activity.
- Case-study, before-and-after, and local-search campaign.
- Existing-customer referral or appointment event.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** In a pressured new-build market, retailers and their agencies need more reliable renovation, replacement, and partial-refit demand. They often operate through disconnected content, promotions, community activity, referrals, and ad hoc messaging, and cannot reliably decide what to do next or learn from campaign results.

**Why alternatives fall short:** CRM begins after a lead exists. Generic AI writing tools create assets without tying them to an offer, entry point, success metric, or reusable learning. Generic marketing platforms lack the local measurement, consultation, and measurement-appointment context of these retailers.

**What it costs them:** Wasted content and promotion spend, inconsistent agency delivery, missed learning, low trust in marketing, and repeated reinvention of the same campaign.

**Emotional tension:** "We keep trying things, but do not know which effort actually brings the next serious customer."

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** No confirmed direct product in the current research set combines vertical campaign playbooks, execution packs, light lead-quality feedback, and a knowledge-learning loop for these retail categories.

**Secondary:** CRM and SCRM products manage leads after capture; generic AI marketing tools create ideas and copy; ad platforms manage media buying; home-improvement marketplaces sell or match demand.

**Indirect:** Spreadsheets, WeChat groups, personal operator experience, templates stored in chat histories, and manual monthly reports.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Starts with a local acquisition playbook rather than a customer database.
- Uses verified, versioned operating knowledge and records applicability, failure conditions, and results.
- Treats the activity's key value behaviour as configurable: qualified consultation, store visit, measurement appointment, or another agreed action.
- Delivers an end-to-end campaign pack: offer, content, local messaging, lead entry, execution checklist, and review record.
- Lets agencies turn their own methods into reusable delivery assets without forcing a full CRM replacement.

**How we do it differently:** AI is grounded in approved playbooks and a retailer brief. It should state its assumptions, use only evidence-backed templates, and flag uncertainty or human-review requirements.

**Why that's better:** Customers receive an executable action and a learning loop rather than generic suggestions or a heavy sales-management implementation.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "We already have a CRM." | Keep it. This product sits before CRM: it plans, launches, and learns from acquisition activities. |
| "AI-generated materials do not understand our business." | The output is constrained by a vertical playbook, the retailer brief, and human approval; it is not a blank prompt. |
| "We already have our own agency templates." | Import and version them. The product helps the agency standardise, localise, measure, and improve its own method. |
| "Can you guarantee customers?" | No. We make activities traceable and improve the odds through fast experimentation; customer volume also depends on execution, local demand, and sales capacity. |

**Anti-persona:** Businesses with no repeatable offer, no ability to handle consultations, no willingness to execute local campaigns, or an expectation of guaranteed leads without feedback.

## Market Thesis
The first commercial wedge is not "all building-material stores". It is growth-oriented bathroom and balcony-cabinet retailers or regional dealers that already spend time or budget on local acquisition and need to make that spend more accountable. The product should prioritise stock-home renovation, old-home refit, local space-improvement, and newly delivered community scenarios rather than depend on broad new-home demand.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Repeated content and promotion work with unclear results; agency delivery that depends on individual experience; client reporting that relies on screenshots and anecdotes.

**Pull:** A clear weekly action, reusable playbook, faster campaign launch, and a defensible client review.

**Habit:** Existing spreadsheets, WeChat groups, personal templates, and a belief that marketing success is purely intuition.

**Anxiety:** The system might be another generic AI writer, require excessive data entry, or fail to fit local and product-specific realities.

## Customer Language
**How they describe the problem:**
- "下一批客户从哪里来？"
- "拍了视频，但不知道有没有真正的装修客户。"
- "每次活动都从头做，做完也不知道有没有用。"
- "客户问效果，我们只能发截图。"
- "会做的人忙不过来，新人又不会做。"

**Words to use:** 获客动作, 打法, 活动, 本地客户, 有效咨询, 到店, 量尺, 复盘, 经验沉淀, 可复制。

**Words to avoid:** 保证爆单, 自动成交, 一键获客, 全自动私信, 万能营销, 替代销售。

## Brand Voice
**Tone:** Grounded, practical, calm, evidence-aware.
**Style:** Direct and action-oriented; show the next campaign rather than abstract theory.
**Personality:** Local, disciplined, helpful, honest about uncertainty, and operationally rigorous.

## Proof Points
**Metrics to earn:** Time from brief to approved campaign; number of campaigns launched; qualified consultations / store visits / measurement appointments; cost per key value behaviour; repeat usage of a playbook; agency delivery time per client; renewal rate.

**Value themes:**
| Theme | Proof to build |
|-------|----------------|
| Faster delivery | Same campaign type launched faster and with fewer operator hours |
| Better local relevance | Content, offer, and campaign entry use local and product-specific context |
| Learning compounds | A playbook improves after each recorded campaign, including failed cases |
| Defensible service value | Agency can show activity, result, cost, and next decision to the retailer |

## Goals
**Business goal:** Validate a paid, repeatable vertical acquisition-delivery workflow before broadening to additional categories.

**Conversion action:** A paid co-creation pilot with a retailer or agency that can launch real campaigns and return activity feedback.

**Current metrics:** Not yet established. Initial validation needs 3 paid pilots, at least 10 launched activities, one playbook reused across more than one client, and at least one renewal or paid expansion signal.
